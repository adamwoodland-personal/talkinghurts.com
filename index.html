Move between devices

Everything this app learns stays on this device. Export a backup file here and import it on your other phone or computer - imports merge, so learning from both devices adds together. The file holds your learned phrases, recent history, name, emergency number and saved places with their locations, so keep it somewhere private.

About

Talking Hurts is a free communication aid for anyone who temporarily can't speak - laryngitis, a strained voice, recovery from throat or thyroid surgery, mouth ulcers, radiotherapy, or just a day when every word costs too much. It learns as you use it: every sentence you speak becomes a one-tap suggestion the next time, organised by where you are. Install it to your home screen and it works entirely offline.

Just lost your voice? Read how to talk without talking, with the NHS's advice.

Does my data leave my device?

Not unless you switch on online voices. Your learned phrases, history, name, settings and saved places stay on your device, in your browser's local storage, and the only other network use is downloading the app itself and, if you choose one, a neural voice. Online voices are off by default: if you allow them in Settings, each sentence you speak may be sent to that voice's provider (such as Google or Microsoft) to be spoken. Backups are files you export and keep yourself.

Does it work offline?

Yes - add it to your home screen and everything works with no connection, including a downloaded neural voice. Prediction runs entirely on the device.

What do the voices sound like?

Out of the box it uses a voice built into your device that works offline (online voices only if you allow them in Settings). If there is none, your sentence still shows in big text. You can optionally download a natural neural voice (Piper, about 60 MB, one-time) that speaks with proper sentence rhythm - much nicer for talking to people all day.

Should I whisper instead?

No. The NHS's advice for laryngitis is to "try to speak as little as possible" and "do not talk loudly or whisper – both strain your voice". That's what Talking Hurts is for: tap, and your phone speaks for you.

Can it help in an emergency?

The red Help tab is always first, with phrases such as "I need help", "Call an ambulance please" and "This is an emergency". Add your emergency number in Settings and it also offers "Call [number] please". It speaks to the people around you; it can't make a call itself.

Is it a medical device?

No. It is a simple free tool, not a regulated medical device or a substitute for professional speech therapy or AAC assessment. For long-term speech loss, please talk to a speech-language professional about full AAC solutions.
