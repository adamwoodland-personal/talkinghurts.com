Lost your voice? How to talk without talking

Laryngitis, a strained voice, or the days after throat surgery: when talking hurts, every word costs something. You can get through a day without saying much at all. Here's what the NHS says to do, and the simplest ways to keep talking to people without using your voice.

This page isn't medical advice. Everything medical on it is quoted from the NHS. If you're worried about your voice, speak to a pharmacist or a GP.

What the NHS says

For laryngitis, the NHS's advice includes:

  • "try to speak as little as possible"
  • "do not talk loudly or whisper – both strain your voice"
  • "drink plenty of fluids"

It says laryngitis "usually goes away on its own after 1 to 2 weeks and you do not need to see a GP". See a GP if "your symptoms do not improve after 2 weeks" or "you keep getting laryngitis or voice problems".

It also says when to get help sooner: contact 111 if "it's very painful or it's difficult to swallow", and call 999 or go to A&E if "you or your child are having difficulty breathing". Those are UK numbers; elsewhere, use your local health line and emergency number.

Ways to get by without your voice

  • Let your phone speak. A text-to-speech app says your words out loud at a normal volume, so you don't have to whisper or croak. Talking Hurts is one, and it's free.
  • Show it in big text. At a counter or somewhere noisy, holding up a sentence in big letters is often quicker than any voice.
  • Write or type. A notepad or your phone's notes app is fine for anything long.
  • Yes, no and gestures. Nods, a thumbs up and pointing cover a surprising amount. Ask people to put things as yes-or-no questions.
  • Say it once, up front. Start with "Sorry, I've lost my voice, I'm using this to talk". People slow down and wait.

Set up Talking Hurts for a day without a voice

  1. Open talkinghurts.com and add it to your home screen, so it works with no signal.
  2. Pick the tab for where you are: Out, Home, Shop, Café or Medical. Each has ready-made phrases, and the Medical tab starts with "I have laryngitis so I can't speak". The red Help tab is always first.
  3. Tap a phrase to say it in one tap, or tap words to build your own sentence and press Speak. Everything you say becomes a one-tap suggestion next time, in the tab where you said it.
  4. In Settings, add your name (for "My name is …") and your emergency number (for "Call … please" on the Help tab).
  5. For a more natural voice, download one of the neural voices over Wi-Fi (about 60 MB, once). Otherwise your phone's built-in voice is used, if it has one that works offline.
  6. Somewhere loud? Press Show and turn the phone round: your sentence fills the screen.

After throat or mouth surgery

After surgery on your throat, voice box, thyroid or mouth, follow your surgeon's or speech and language therapist's instructions on how long to rest your voice and what's allowed. Talking Hurts doesn't change that advice; it makes the quiet days easier, so you can still order a coffee, answer the door or tell a nurse what you need.

For long-term or permanent loss of speech, a speech and language therapist can assess you for a full AAC (augmentative and alternative communication) system. Talking Hurts is a simple free tool, not a medical device and not a replacement for that.

Questions

Should I whisper instead?

No. The NHS's advice for laryngitis is to "try to speak as little as possible" and "do not talk loudly or whisper – both strain your voice". That's what Talking Hurts is for: tap, and your phone speaks for you.

How long does laryngitis last?

The NHS says it "usually goes away on its own after 1 to 2 weeks". See a GP if your symptoms do not improve after 2 weeks, or if you keep getting laryngitis or voice problems.

Is Talking Hurts free?

Yes. There's no account, no adverts and no tracking. Your phrases and settings stay on your device, and nothing you say leaves it unless you switch on online voices.

Does it work without signal?

Yes. Add it to your home screen and it works with no connection, including a neural voice you've downloaded.
